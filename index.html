Method & limitations

  • Valuation date. Everything is valued as of the deal close (December 2025 in the Bollsta example). Cash flows are modelled quarterly and discounted back to that date in nominal USD.
  • Cash flow = EBITDA − maintenance capex. Maintenance capex is an assumption of USD 10m per year. There is no separate special periodic survey cost.
  • Unlevered view. Debt flows are left out (the Bollsta purchase was fully debt financed). We value the asset itself and discount at an asset-level (WACC-style) rate. How the buyer paid does not change what the rig is worth.
  • Taxes ignored. Rig-owning entities typically sit in low-tax structures, so this is a simplification.
  • Residual value. The slider sets the rig's value at the end of the firm contract. Each option year exercised adds a year of age, and the value falls 4% per year (assumption). The rig is sold at the end of the last contract year. There is no idle time or recontracting beyond the options.
  • Payback counts operating cash only and excludes the residual value.
  • Synergies (Bollsta example). Odfjell already operated the rig before the acquisition, so as owner it keeps the full margin with no handover cost. A strategic buyer like this can justify a higher price than a financial buyer could.
  • Inflation (Bollsta example). USD 400m in 2017 is roughly USD 520–530m in 2025 dollars (US CPI). In real terms Odfjell paid less than Northern Ocean did.
  • Reported figures (Bollsta example). The 2017 price and the Deepsea Mira construction cost come from a secondary source.
